Free, open kit · AL1–AL5 ladder

Do you know which rung AI is on in your work?

Almost everyone thinks they use AI a lot, but can’t say which tasks it already handles on its own and which still pass through their hands. The AL1–AL5 ladder measures this task by task, on paper or automatically, and shows which task can move up a rung now, before you hand too much over to an AI that still makes mistakes, or keep doing by hand what it already does well.

Understand it in 5 minutes View on GitHub
In short

Without measuring, you don’t know what you still do by hand, what AI already does on its own, or which doors you’ve opened to it. This kit gives you a five-rung scale, from AL1 (you do it yourself) to AL5 (AI runs on its own), and two ways to measure: a paper sheet that works with any person and any AI, and a program that reads Claude Code and Codex logs (AI assistants that work on your computer) and records each connection used. If you only use ChatGPT or another AI in a browser, the sheet is enough. At the end, you’ll know which rung each task is on, which one can move up now, and when to move down. To get started, just print the sheet.

The ladder: five rungs

AL means autonomy level: how much AI does without you. The rung applies to one task, not to a person or an AI. You might be at AL5 for translating captions and AL2 for answering a customer. Example across all rungs: replying to a customer’s email.

AL1

You do it yourself

AI isn’t involved.

You read, think, write, and send.

AL2

AI helps

You ask, it answers, you do the work.

You ask, “How should I reply to this?” and write the response.

AL3

AI collaborates

You work together, step by step. You guide each step.

AI writes a draft with you; you adjust and send it.

AL4

AI leads, you review

It prepares everything; you check before it goes out.

The response is ready; you read and approve it, then AI sends it.

AL5

AI runs on its own

Schedule, rules, and alerts, with no one in the middle.

Routine questions are answered on their own; you review the summary and exceptions.

When in doubt, ask three questions

1. Was AI involved? No: AL1.   2. Who did the work? You, using its answer: AL2. Both of you, step by step: AL3. AI did it, and you checked before it went out: AL4.   3. Were you there? No, it ran on its own: AL5.

How to move up a level

One task at a time, one rung at a time. Each time you do the task, record the date, whether it’s critical, the rung you worked at, and two quality columns on the sheet: Fixed? (n = no changes needed, p = small fix, g = big fix or the answer wasn’t helpful) and Serious error? (y/n). Only big fixes and serious errors count, and only the times at your current rung: when you change rungs, the count starts over. You decide: the report checks the calculations and gives one of four recommendations: “move up,” “record N more times,” “stay on this rung,” or “move down”; if you disagree, your decision stands, with the reason recorded. The numbers are a starting point.

AL1→AL2

Start asking

Choose a task you repeat at least 3 times a month that doesn’t involve confidential data.

Do this: before doing it by hand, ask AI how it would do it and compare its approach with yours. From that point on, you’re at AL2: record AL2 on the sheet.

Ready when: No criteria. Asking has low risk; tracking starts at AL2.

AL2→AL3

Give it context and work together

Signal: You copy the answer and always make the same adjustments.

Do this: write down the fixed context once (who the customer is, the tone, good examples, where the files are) and include it with your request.

Ready when, of the last 10 times, no more than 1 required a big fix (g).

AL3→AL4

Let AI lead, then review

Signal: You approve almost everything it suggests without changes.

Do this: write a short checklist defining what “ready” means. AI does the entire task and stops before sending, publishing, or paying. You check it against the list and approve.

Ready when, of the last 20 times, no more than 2 required a big fix.

AL4→AL5

Let it run on its own, with safeguards

Before you let it run: set a schedule, time and cost limits, an error alert, a record of everything it does, and a way to return to AL4 in one minute.

Afterward: review 3 results each week and record them on the sheet.

Ready when, of the last 50 times, no more than 2 required a big fix and none had a serious error.

🛑 Critical tasks stop at AL4

When an error could cause public, financial, or security harm (publishing, money, servers, customers), AI leads and you review. It doesn’t move to AL5. Mark “critical? = y” on the sheet. If the column is blank, the program decides based on words in the task name (publish, post, customer, payment, money, server, email); anything you mark, y or n, overrides the words.

⬇️ When to move down a rung

One serious error (something incorrect went out, data was lost, or more was spent than intended): move down right away. Three big fixes in the last 10 times: move down. Since moving up allows at most two, the rules never conflict. If the tool or process changed, move down one rung on your own until you measure again. This applies at any rung, including AL5.

How to measure: on paper or automatically

You can only move up what you can see. Without a record, the rung is just a guess.

📝 On paper

A sheet you can print. For each task, record the date, task, whether it’s critical, rung (1 to 5), minutes, connection used (which AI, program, website, or folder), whether you needed to make a correction (n/p/g), and whether there was a serious error. It works with any AI and is the only way to count AL1, the work you do without AI. Open the printable sheet.

🤖 Automatically

For people who use Claude Code or Codex (AI assistants that work on your computer). A program reads their logs, assigns each conversation to a rung, and records every connection used: programs, websites, external services, AI helpers, and ready-made recipes (skills), with the first and last time each was used. It can run on its own every day. How to use ↓

🔗 The same columns

The paper sheet and program use the same columns. Start on paper today and enter the sheet into a spreadsheet whenever you’re ready: the report combines both, including connections you recorded (ChatGPT, spreadsheets, email).

QuestionWhere it appears

Real case: Nei’s measured ladder

Nei Maldaner (INEMA) ran the meter on his own records from the last 30 days, through 11/12/2026: 16,228 work sessions and 133,885 AI actions. 1,057 sessions were conversations with him; the others were started by scripts, bots, and schedules. In the 12/11 video, he showed 64%, measured with the relogio-2027 kit, which uses a different yardstick. This kit counts as “runs on its own” only a session started by a script, bot, or schedule with up to 2 messages; if you exchanged 3 or more messages, it counts as a conversation, even if it was through the app or the bot. By this yardstick, it comes to 56.2%. The number changes with the yardstick and the time window: that’s why the yardstick is written down and the measurement is repeated every day.

Certificate

Show which rung you are on and how far you have climbed

The kit turns your measurements into a one-page certificate: the % of work conducted by AI, the AL1 to AL5 ladder, the main tasks with the next rung, your week-by-week progress, and the projection for 1, 3, and 6 months. You choose the period (30, 90 days, or more). It is an HTML page: open it in any browser and turn it into a PDF with the “Save as PDF” button, without image software.

What it proves, and what it does not: it is a measurement recorded from your own machine’s records, not an official assessment. It lets you show your current standing and track progress from one measurement to the next. In the example, Nei over 90 days: he went from 11% to 61% of work conducted by AI. During that period, the Codex records start on Aug 16 and the Claude Code records on Aug 25.

Two ways to run it, and what happens to your data

The meter is a Python program that runs only on your computer: it reads the Claude Code and Codex records already on your machine, does not connect to the internet, and saves the result in the registro/ folder. The difference is who reads the report afterward.

🔒 A. In the terminal

You run the commands (step by step) and open the certificate in your browser. Nothing leaves your computer. Use this option if your data is confidential: client work, an NDA, or a project that cannot be disclosed.

💬 B. Ask your AI

Claude Code or Codex runs the same commands and then reads the report to summarize it for you. That reading becomes part of the conversation, and the conversation goes through the provider’s server (Anthropic or OpenAI), like any other session. The report shows the names of your tasks and the connections used: programs, agents, skills, and sites the AI accessed. The text of your requests is stored only in registro/sessoes.json, and the AI does not need to open that file. These names may reveal the name of a client or project.

Option B, the easiest: ask your AI

Open Claude Code or Codex on your computer, paste the request below (replace the name), and wait a few minutes. It downloads the kit, measures your activity, generates the certificate, and opens it in your browser.

Download the kit from https://github.com/inematds/nivel-ia into a folder named nivel-ia (use it if it already exists), measure my last 30 days with python3 medir/nivel.py varrer --dias 30 and python3 medir/nivel.py relatorio --lang en, generate my certificate with python3 medir/certificado.py --nome "Your Name" --lang en, and open the certificate in the browser. At the end, tell me where the certificate and report were saved and summarize the results in 3 lines. Do not open the file registro/sessoes.json and do not send my data to any other service or website.

Want 90 days? Add this to your request: "measure 90 days in a separate folder (--registro /tmp/n90), without changing the daily log". First, keep in mind: Claude Code deletes records older than 30 days unless you increase cleanupPeriodDays in its settings (e.g., to 365) and wait for the history to accumulate. Anything already deleted cannot be recovered. If the records cover fewer days than you requested, the meter will warn you in the report and certificate.

Technical section

The kit: for those who want to measure on their own computer

You don’t need this section to understand what came before. The paper sheet is enough.

What’s in the kit

🪜 ESCADA.md (in Portuguese)

The five rungs, the three questions, how to move up each rung, the cap for the critical task, and when to move down.

📝 Paper sheet

kit/ficha-papel.html to print (landscape A4) and kit/papel-modelo.csv for typing, with the same columns.

🤖 Meter

medir/nivel.py: reads the logs, classifies each session, combines the sheet, records connections, and writes the report. Python standard library only, no network, no API.

How it works

Claude Code and Codex logs→scan→each session’s rung + connections→+ paper sheet→report→you decide who moves up
What’s in the recordRung
Session started by a script, bot, or schedule (claude -p, codex exec) with up to 2 messages; 3 or more = conversationAL5
Your request sent by a bot (for example, if you talk to AI through Telegram): AI handles the entire request, and you review the resultAL4
Conversation with 15 or more AI actions per message from youAL4
5 to 14 actions per message from youAL3
Fewer than 5 actions per message from you (includes a standalone question)AL2
What you do without AIAL1 (sheet only)

The thresholds are at the top of medir/nivel.py (LIMITE_AL4, LIMITE_AL3). Tasks are inferred from words in the first request of each session; to use your own, copy medir/tarefas.exemplo.json to medir/tarefas.json and edit it (each line: task name, whether it is critical, identifying words). Moving up and down is checked against the sheet: up to 1 big fix in the last 10 times to reach AL3, up to 2 in 20 for AL4, up to 2 in 50 and no serious errors for AL5; 1 serious error or 3 big fixes in the last 10 times = move down. Folders and files used are recorded only on the sheet: the program records programs, sites, services, helpers, and skills.

Prerequisites

Terminal + Python 3.9+

Type the commands below in the Terminal (on Mac: Applications › Utilities › Terminal; on Linux: Ctrl+Alt+T). Python uses only the standard library, nothing to install. On Windows, use WSL (Linux inside Windows).

python3 --version

git (or the ZIP)

To download the kit. Without git, download the ZIP: nivel-ia-main.zip, extract it, and open the Terminal inside the folder.

Claude Code or Codex

The meter reads ~/.claude/projects and ~/.codex*/sessions. Without them, use only the sheet: skip steps 2 and 4, do step 5, and run step 3; the report will include only the sheet.

systemd (optional)

The Linux scheduler, to run automatically every day at 06:20.

How to use

1

Download the kit

One folder, no installation. Without git, use the ZIP from the prerequisites.

git clone https://github.com/inematds/nivel-ia && cd nivel-ia
2

Measure the last 30 days

Reads the logs on this machine and saves registro/sessoes.json (outside git).

python3 medir/nivel.py varrer --dias 30   # "16701 sessões (9820 Claude Code, 6881 Codex) em 30 dias"
3

Read the report

Ladder, tasks, next rung, who can move up or down, and connections. It’s saved in registro/relatorio.md. Background sessions from other programs are excluded, so the report counts slightly fewer than the scan. Note: the report, the command output and the column names of kit/papel-modelo.csv are in Portuguese (data, tarefa, nivel, minutos, conexao, corrigi, grave); the program also accepts the English names on the sheet (date, task, level, minutes, connection, fixed, serious) and y/yes.

python3 medir/nivel.py relatorio --lang en   # "A IA conduz **56.2%** do trabalho medido (AL4 + AL5)"
4

See all connections

Each program, site, service, agent, and skill, with the number of sessions and the first and last time it appeared.

python3 medir/nivel.py conexoes
5

Combine the paper sheet (and AL1)

Copy the template to the registro/ folder. Open registro/papel.csv in a spreadsheet (Excel, LibreOffice, or Google Sheets), enter one row for each time you did the task, and save it again as CSV. Dates can be dd/mm/yyyy or yyyy-mm-dd; column names can match the printed sheet in Portuguese, English, or Spanish. Then check: the program will warn you if it can’t understand a row or column. The next report will add everything together.

cp kit/papel-modelo.csv registro/papel.csv
python3 medir/nivel.py papel   # "5 linhas válidas em registro/papel.csv"
6

Run automatically every day

Turns on a timer at 06:20 that measures and saves the history in registro/historico.jsonl.

sh systemd/instalar.sh   # undo: systemctl --user disable --now nivel-ia.timer
7

Generate your certificate

A page with your rung, week-by-week progress, and projection. Open the file in a browser and use “Save as PDF”. For a longer period (e.g., 90 days), measure in a separate folder without changing the daily record.

python3 medir/certificado.py --nome "Your Name" --lang en   # saves registro/certificado-AAAA-MM-DD-en.html
# 90 days:
python3 medir/nivel.py --registro /tmp/n90 varrer --dias 90
python3 medir/nivel.py --registro /tmp/n90 relatorio --lang en
python3 medir/certificado.py --nome "Your Name" --lang en --registro /tmp/n90
8

Decide once a week

Choose ONE task to move up a rung using the criterion above, and move down the one with a serious mistake. Kit tests: python3 tests/run.py (46 tests with fake records).

From scratch to a PDF certificate

You need Linux or Mac (on Windows, use WSL), Python 3.9 or newer (python3 --version), and you must have used Claude Code or Codex on this machine, because the certificate is generated from their logs. If you only use ChatGPT in a browser, use the paper form.

git clone https://github.com/inematds/nivel-ia

Track progress over time (optional)

Measures automatically every day at 06:20. Generate a new certificate whenever you want (step 4): with more weeks of data, the chart and projection improve.

sh systemd/instalar.sh

What the measurement doesn’t see

AL1 and other AIs

What you do without AI and what you do in ChatGPT in a browser aren’t in the logs. Record them on the sheet.

Accuracy

The rung shows how much freedom AI had, not whether it got things right. Accuracy is tracked in the “Fixed?” and “Serious error?” columns.

Time

This is time spent working with AI; parallel sessions are added together. Use it to compare tasks, not to count your hours.

Privacy: nothing leaves your machine. The first request in each session is saved briefly, with keys, email addresses, and long numbers replaced by [oculto], and the registro/ folder stays outside git.
